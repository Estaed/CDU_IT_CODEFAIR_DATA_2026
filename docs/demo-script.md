# The thirty-second demo

Written 2026-09-21 (PRD §4.2, fifth batch). One sentence, one community, three taps. The
report's introduction, the first slide and the app's home screen all use the same sentence;
do not paraphrase it.

## The sentence

> Coverage maps say there is signal. We ask whether the clinic there can run a video call.

## What Crosscheck is, if someone asks

A diagnosis, not a fix. Public sources about 96 remote NT communities disagree with each
other and none of them says what the connection lets people do. Crosscheck puts them side by
side, says per community which services work and why not, and ranks where to act first and
who should act. It measures nothing itself; every figure carries its source and date.

## The script (about 30 seconds)

1. **Open with what is at stake (8 s).** "In March, Kintore lost mobile service for six days.
   People could not pay for fuel, top up prepaid power, or call Triple Zero. In Wadeye it was
   nine weeks of rolling outages." *(Sources below. Say the places and the numbers, nothing
   more.)*
2. **The sentence (5 s).** "Coverage maps say there is signal. We ask whether the clinic there
   can run a video call."
3. **Phone in flight mode, open the app (3 s).** The home screen shows the same sentence and
   three numbers. Read one: "A video call is known to work in 1 of 70 clinics."
4. **Tap `Find a community` → Wadeye (7 s).** Read the summary line aloud: "All four sources
   say Wadeye has mobile coverage. A video call with a doctor is not proven to work here:
   664.9 ms on satellite against the 100 ms it needs." Tap the row: the two figures and
   their sources.
5. **Tap `Fix first` (7 s).** "96 communities, ranked, each with what to do and who should do
   it. This is the list the department can act on. When a publisher updates its data we
   rerun the pipeline and the row changes: the same tool checks whether the money worked."

If there is time: the map, then `Update another phone by camera` on Share as the last thing,
never the first.

## Words to use and to avoid

| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| sources, maps | publishers, publisher lines |
| how far to trust the map | claim reliability, p_wrong |
| what to fix first | priority score, intervention |
| the best connection the community can get | best available path |
| not proven to work | degraded |

## Sources for step 1 (checked 2026-09-21)

  Triple Zero calls": https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-03-27/telstra-outage-in-kintore-nt-impacting-power-fuel-and-triple0/106499742
- ABC News, 2026-04-07, "Remote NT community of Wadeye experiences weeks of rolling Telstra
  outages": https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-04-07/nt-community-wadeye-experiences-weeks-of-rolling-telstra-outages/106535008

Telstra's position in both articles is that satellite Triple Zero stayed available; residents
reported otherwise. If challenged, say exactly that.

## The hand-over test

Give the phone to someone who has not seen it, say nothing, and after ten seconds ask "what
is this for?". Three of three answering in the sense of the sentence above is the pass mark.
Record who, when and what they said in `reports/` before Challenge Day.
